Where Found

Sri Lanka, known as 'Ratna Dweepa', holds vast deposits of precious stones. The gem-rich alluvial gravels, locally known as 'Illam', are primarily found in Ratnapura, Elahera, and Balangoda regions, which are world-renowned for their geological wealth.

Formation

Gemstones are formed over millions of years deep within the earth's crust. Through extreme geological processes—intense heat and high pressure—mineral-rich rocks transform into crystalline structures. Most Sri Lankan gems originate from metamorphic rocks, weathered and transported by water into alluvial deposits.

Gem Industry

The Sri Lankan gem industry is a cornerstone of the national economy, globally celebrated for ethical mining practices, traditional artisan cutting, and precision faceting. It encompasses a complete value chain from local gem pits to high-end international trade, ensuring sustainability and exquisite craftsmanship.

Gemstone Details

Blue Sapphire: This is a very strong and valuable gemstone that represents wisdom and loyalty. It is famous for its deep, royal blue color and is very popular for high-quality jewelry because it is hard enough for daily use.
Ruby: Known as the "king of gems," it is celebrated for its intense and bright red color. It is extremely durable and is often chosen for special pieces like engagement rings because of its lasting value.
Cat's Eye: This stone is truly unique because of its special light effect called "chatoyancy". When you look at it, a thin line of light moves across the surface, which looks exactly like the eye of a cat.
Alexandrite: This is a rare and mysterious gemstone that has the amazing ability to change its color. It appears green in natural sunlight but turns to a reddish-purple color when seen under indoor lamp light.
Spinel: Spinel is a wonderful, natural gemstone that is often appreciated for its wide variety of bright colors. Because it has great clarity and is very durable, it is a fantastic and high-quality alternative to more expensive stones.
Garnet: Many people think garnets are only dark red, but they actually come in almost every color of the rainbow. It is an ancient stone that symbolizes energy and lasting friendship, making it a meaningful gift.
Tourmaline: This is often called the "rainbow gemstone" because of its incredible range of colors. Sometimes, a single piece of tourmaline can even show two or three different colors at the same time, which makes it very popular for artistic jewelry.
Zircon: Zircon is one of the oldest minerals on Earth and is known for its incredible sparkle. It has a high ability to reflect light, which gives it a brilliance that is very similar to a diamond.
Moonstone: This stone looks very soft, magical, and elegant. It has a glowing, milky sheen that seems to float across the surface, which reminds people of the soft light of the moon at night.

Where Found

Sri Lanka is globally recognized as the "Ratna Dweepa" (Gem Island). The primary sources of these precious stones are the secondary alluvial deposits known as 'Illam'. The most significant gem-bearing regions are Ratnapura (the City of Gems), Elahera, and Balangoda. These areas contain rich gravels deposited over aeons by ancient river systems, where heavy gemstones settle and are harvested using both traditional and modern mining methods.

Formation

The creation of gemstones is a complex geological process that spans millions of years. Most of Sri Lanka's high-quality gemstones are formed within metamorphic rocks such as marble and granulite. These rocks are subjected to extreme geothermal heat and immense tectonic pressure deep within the earth's crust, causing minerals to recrystallize into beautiful, stable geometric structures. Over time, erosion wears down these host rocks, and water currents transport the resistant, hard gemstones into the riverbeds where they are eventually discovered.

Gem Industry

The Sri Lankan gem industry represents a sophisticated and highly sustainable sector. It is built upon a legacy of centuries-old mining techniques combined with state-of-the-art lapidary technology. The industry adheres to ethical mining practices that prioritize environmental conservation. From the initial mining and sorting stages to precision cutting, heat treatment for color enhancement, and final certification, Sri Lanka maintains an end-to-end supply chain that serves international luxury markets worldwide.
